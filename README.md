### 🏊 Previously on Federico: the pool years

Before staring at terminals, I spent about 15 years staring at a black line at the bottom of a pool.
Distance freestyle (400 / 800 / 1500 m) — the discipline for people who find marathons too short and too dry.

- 🇮🇹 **Olympian** — Beijing 2008. Fun fact: Michael Phelps never beat me in any of my races. (He never entered them, but let's not get lost in details.)
- 🥇 **2× European champion** — two golds, and still the only European standard I've ever met on the first try (sorry, GDPR)
- 🥉 **World Championships bronze** — 800 m free, Melbourne 2007. Still the best 7'50" of my life; no deploy has beaten it yet
- ⏱️ **Former European record holder** in the 800 m free — the record lasted longer than most of my side projects
- 🏆 **23 Italian titles**, mostly in the 1500 m, which is basically a 15-minute meditation with lactic acid

Full career in [Wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Colbertaldo), if you need proof that I once had abs.

### 💼 Then I dried off

I've been **CEO, General Manager and CTO** of a few companies in tech and tourism.
Since apparently that left way too much free time, I also moonlight as:

```yaml
federico:
  roles:
    - IT consultant      # "have you tried turning it off and on again?" but billed
    - sysadmin           # talks to servers more than to humans
    - DevOps engineer    # it works on my VPS
    - solution architect # draws boxes and arrows, then has to build them
    - developer          # writes the code that the architect promised
  on_call: always        # hotels don't close, and neither does the PBX
  sleep: best-effort
```

In practice: I design the infrastructure for hotels, restaurants and small businesses — then
I personally crimp the cable, configure the PBX, deploy the stack and get the 3 AM alert.
Full-stack in the most literal, slightly worrying sense.

### 🛠️ Things I break and then fix

- 🖥️ **Infrastructure** — from bare metal to cloud and back: dedicated servers, VPS fleets, hypervisors (Proxmox, Hyper-V, ESXi), containers, and the occasional server hidden under a hotel reception desk. Hardening, observability, off-site backups and disaster recovery plans — tested *before* the disaster. Usually.
- 🌐 **Networking** — L2/L3 design, VLAN segmentation and inter-VLAN ACLs, stateful firewalls, site-to-site VPNs and zero-trust overlays, QoS for voice, IGMP snooping for multicast IPTV, mDNS reflection across subnets, seamless WiFi roaming, rogue DHCP hunting. In short: packets go where they should, and the printer nobody could find finally shows up on every VLAN.
- 📞 **VoIP & telephony** — Asterisk/FreePBX and 3CX, SIP trunks, failover routing, zero-touch provisioning. I've read more SIP traces than novels.
- 🔐 **Security** — pentests and Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace / Active Directory audits. I find the problem; you decide whether to panic.
- 💻 **Web dev** — TypeScript, Next.js and React, Node, Python/Flask, Laravel, PostgreSQL with pgvector, Redis, Prisma: SaaS platforms and CRMs nobody asked for but everyone ends up using. …and yes, WordPress too. Someone has to keep 40% of the internet alive.
- 📈 **Analytics** — server-side GTM and GA4, so marketing can finally blame the right thing
- 🤖 **AI & automation** — putting AI to work in real operations, not just in slide decks: LLM agents with tool use, RAG over company knowledge, AI voice agents answering the phone system, document extraction and transcription pipelines, marketing agents, plus a squad of coding agents I orchestrate like a dev team that never asks for holidays (or a raise).

…except this one, which escaped:

- 🏊 **[sh-tafeta](https://github.com/ScIEnzY/sh-tafeta)** — a pun on `sh` and *staffetta* (Italian for relay race), the way my coach Bane used to say it: "shtafeta". Nobody stops swimming: one touches the wall and the next one is already in the water. It resumes every Claude Code session after a reboot, on WSL and remote servers: one Windows Terminal tab per tmux session, safe two-way sync, and screenshots that reach the server with a plain Ctrl+V. Born because Windows kept crashing (thanks, Microsoft) and taking my dev team of AI agents down with it, mid-sprint.
